# Positioning, Title Meaning & Comparables

*Reference doc distilled from the 2026-07-24 PRD-vision party session (Mary + Samus, with Zach). Captures the "why/pitch" of TKYDS: what the title means, the core-fantasy spine, the logline, the market comparables and their transferable lessons, and the two positioning risks. See also `design-multipath-routes-framework.md` (the "how" of distinct routes) and `design-session-2026-07-24-social-political-layer.md` (the systems).*

---

## 1. The title is an arc-vector (governing idea)

**"The King You Don't See" is not a stack of co-equal meanings — it is a single vector with a start and an end.** The unseen controlling actor is the *same entity* at both ends of the game:

> At the start there is a "king you don't see" — an actor so far above you that you cannot even perceive, let alone reach, the hand that shapes how the kingdom behaves and how decisions cascade culturally downward. The game is the **climb to become that actor** — the one whose decisions propagate across the world.

The two other readings are **the capabilities you accumulate along that vector**, not sibling meanings:

- **Perception** (the *entry* capability): at the start you can't even *see* the hand steering the cascades. Learning to read the hidden web (greeting rungs, sigils, tells) is beat one of the climb.
- **Reach** (the *exit* capability): by the end your decisions propagate the way theirs did. Reach is what you've been buying the whole time.

So the arc — *see the hidden board → reach through it → rise until you are the board* — **is** the title. The arc is path-agnostic, which is exactly what a multi-path game needs its title to be: some become the propagating actor by whispering, some by commanding armies.

**Positioning risk from the multiplicity:** if you *pitch* the ambiguity ("it means many things!"), players read "the devs haven't decided." **The multiplicity must be discovered by playing, never pitched as ambiguity.** Sell one loop; let the title's echoes reward the people who look.

## 2. The core-fantasy spine: illegible authorship (not "unseen")

The mechanical spine is **illegible authorship of consequence** — the private thrill of causing outcomes the world can never fully read back to you. Named as a feeling: *"I made that happen — and the world can't fully read how."*

**Retire "unseen" as the mechanical definition.** "The King You Don't See" names illegibility of **reach and method**, not physical invisibility — real kings aren't invisible. The hidden manipulator and the overt soldier-commanding warlord are *both* unseen in the sense that matters: the world never fully reads the apparatus of power behind them. The sword is visible; the reason the sword obeys is not.

Consequence: **direct/overt play is first-class, not an apology.** "Unseen" becomes the *purest expression* of the spine (the manipulator end of a spectrum), never its definition. The through-line across all playstyles is **the gap between what the world attributes to you and what you actually authored** — every path is a different-sized version of that gap, and that gap is the fun.

**Open design note (attribution).** For the epic emergent moment, Zach's call: the sim does **not** need to mark the counterfactual "pivot node" where your influence was decisive. Authorship is delivered by **predict-then-watch** — the player causes the pivotal decision (directly or via an agent) and *predicts* the cascade, then sees it land. The visible traceable chain + the player's own prediction is the authorship; the exact % that tipped a decision doesn't matter.

## 3. Definition of power (verbatim, load-bearing)

> **Power is a measure of how many people fall under the shadow of your decisions.**

Not wealth, not territory — causal reach over the choices of others. You use resources to sway people; the resources are never the point. (This is the anchor for the tycoon-trap guardrail in §6.)

---

## 4. The logline

**Shipped (anchors the PRD exec summary):**
> *A living kingdom is steered by an unseen power you begin too small to even see — climb through war, commerce, crime, secrets, public service, and more until you are that power.*

Why it holds: it names the paths (proving multiplicity without pitching ambiguity), states the arc (too-small-to-see → you-are-that-power), the open-ended "and more" leaves room for paths to be added as systems layer in, and "steered… cascade to everyone" keeps *people* in frame (guards the tycoon trap).

**Fuller version (trailer/store):**
> *The King You Don't See is a living medieval kingdom run by an unseen hand whose decisions cascade to everyone — start too powerless to even perceive that hand, and claw upward, through commerce, war, crime, or secrets, until the unseen hand is yours.*

**Alternate (leans harder into become-the-king):**
> *Somewhere above you, an unseen hand decides how the whole kingdom lives. You start as no one who can even perceive it. The game is the climb to replace it — by whatever engine of power you can build.*

## 5. Comparables playbook

The success formula across all comparables: **one substrate + world-state-dependent verb-gating + diegetic reaction that reflects your path back to you.** The failure modes: a dominant "intended" path, or one verb wearing costumes.

### Crusader Kings III — the cleanest success (closest comparable)
All paths read the *same* underlying numbers (opinion, prestige, dread, stats, claims) but **gate different verbs behind different stat thresholds**: high Intrigue unlocks murder/seduce/abduct schemes; high Martial unlocks war; Dread tyrannizes vassals into compliance. Coherence comes from all paths scoring against one currency of consequence (does the vassal comply?).
**Transferable lesson:** distinctness comes from which VERBS a stat-profile unlocks against a shared world-state — build one resolver, gate the action list. TKYDS expresses this as **composed value-chains** instead of skill trees, gating **political propagation power** — a genuinely fresh expression Mary hadn't seen shipped.

### Dishonored — coherence success, parity failure (the warning)
One guard AI, one detection model, one sandbox; Chaos is an *emergent tally* of bodies left, and the **world's reaction** (weepers, darker ending, NPC comments) sells the fork — reactivity, not separate content.
**Transferable lesson:** paths must be legible back through **world-reaction** — TKYDS's diegetic body-language/greeting-rung shifts ARE its Chaos meter, told diegetically.
**The warning (load-bearing):** low-chaos was the "intended/rewarded" path (better ending), so parity was imperfect — high-chaos players felt scolded. **If TKYDS's ethical-savvy path yields the "good ending" and the vicious-commander is framed as its fail-state, you've secretly built one path with a penalty box.** Score every path as legitimately winnable.

### Shadow of Mordor (Nemesis) — emergence without multiplicity (the cautionary tale)
Nemesis brilliantly generates the "traceable emergent moment rivals didn't foresee" (TKYDS's north star for emergence), but the *playstyle* multiplicity is thin — the loop collapses to "brand/dominate the orc."
**Transferable lesson (anti-pattern):** **emergence alone does NOT create path multiplicity.** A rich generator with one dominant verb gives great stories about the *same* playstyle. TKYDS needs Nemesis-grade emergence AND CK3-grade verb-gating, or it becomes "puppet-master: the only viable build."

### Disco Elysium — embodied social legibility without combat execution
Reference for a continuous, embodied world where the skill is reading and social navigation, not execution. Supports TKYDS's "the interface is the world / no combat execution" bet.

## 6. Risk 1 — the tycoon trap (sharpest positioning risk)

Because paths are literally "mine iron, employ smiths, feed the war machine" or "buy real estate, run brothels, buy wholesale and hire merchants," the store screenshot risks looking like *Anno* / *Patrician* / *Software Inc.* — a supply-chain tycoon. If economy reads as the *point*, TKYDS gets shelved next to management sims and the leverage/read-people/propagate-decisions differentiator evaporates.

**The fix — a rule, not a hope:** *The economy is never the win condition; people are. Every economic verb terminates in a person's decision.* You don't accumulate iron to have iron — you accumulate it to employ smiths whose loyalty/dependence you own, arm soldiers who obey, make a rival's supply fail so his vassals' opinion of him collapses.

- **Lead every path description with the person it captures, not the commodity.** Not "control the iron trade" but "make every smith in the province depend on your forge." The commodity is the sentence's object; the person is its point.
- **The victory readout is diegetic and social, never a balance sheet.** A tycoon shows a profit graph; TKYDS shows a duke who now defers to you. The legibility pillar IS the scoreboard.
- **One-line guardrail:** *The player builds economic engines, but the game scores political reach. If a feature lets the player get richer without getting closer to bending a person's decision, it's off-thesis.*

**Believability nuance (Zach):** wealth-without-power must be a *legal state of the world* — not every actor is power-hungry; many NPCs have a profit motive and are content to be wealthy. So the guardrail is off-*progress*, not off-*existence*: any actor (incl. the player) may pursue wealth for its own sake, but wealth only advances the player's **arc** when converted into sway over people.

---

## 7. Risk 2 — path parity (or the multiplicity is mush)

The greatest structural risk after the tycoon trap. Comparables prove multiplicity dies when one path is secretly "the real game" (Dishonored's nudge) or when emergence masks a single verb (Mordor). Commit to a **testable parity claim**: each path must reach a top-tier outcome the others structurally cannot, and be able to compete across routes. If the design can't name a distinct win-condition for the vicious-commander that the puppet-master can't reach, no logline saves it. (See the routes-framework doc §7 for the mechanical side.)
